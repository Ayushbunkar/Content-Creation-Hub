 Across global geopolitics and maritime security, no geographical choke point wields as much raw leverage as the Strait of Hormuz. Separating the rocky shores of Iran from the Arabian Peninsula, this strategic passage narrows to barely 21 nautical miles at its critical gateway. Every single day, over 21 million barrels of petroleum and liquefied natural gas traverse these waters, accounting for more than 20 percent of total global petroleum consumption. Under international maritime law and the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, transit passage is technically guaranteed, yet sovereign posturing and naval mind capabilities create an enduring flashpoint. For the superpowers and global markets alike, any disruption in these contested waters would immediately trigger catastrophic global energy inflation and economic paralysis.
